The problem

Trend maps describe the past. They rarely help someone decide what to do.

Signals studied in isolation

Temperature, rainfall, vegetation and wind are usually mapped one variable at a time, so the links between them stay hidden.

A trend is not a warning

A long-term warming slope cannot, by itself, tell a planner where the next flood will arrive or by when.

Decisions need evidence

Analysts need credible, interpretable context with the uncertainty shown — not opaque single-number risk scores.

The framework

The Earth Hazard Signature

Each assessment is four interpretable layers instead of one opaque score. A warming trend never automatically raises a flood number — any influence must be justified by model testing.

01

Climate trend

Have environmental conditions been changing significantly over 2001–2024?

Theil–Sen slope · Mann–Kendall

Environmental anomaly

Seasonal anomaly · z-scores

Hazard likelihood

Exposure

Which communities, roads or other assets could be affected if the hazard arrives?

Settlement · road network
Honest by design

Three questions, two engines, one honest answer.

  • What has been changing? Statistically significant long-term trends in four NASA variables, with uncertainty.
  • What is happening now? Anomalies, antecedent rainfall and susceptibility combined into current context.
  • What might happen next? A calibrated hazard model with explicit horizons, confidence and SHAP evidence.

A dedicated trend ablation test asks whether long-term trend features actually improve near-term hazard prediction. If they don't, we report that result.
